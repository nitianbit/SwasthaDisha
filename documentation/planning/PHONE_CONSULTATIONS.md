# Basic-phone consultations and partner-assisted top-ups

**Version 1.7, 9 November 2026. Status: user-requested scope; proposed design, not implemented.** A patient without a smartphone can fund a consultation account at an approved pharmacy, laboratory or hospital, then call a Jansetu helpline from a basic phone to speak to an available doctor. This is a top-up of a healthcare account, not mobile-network airtime. No ABHA, app login or smartphone is required for this route. AR-23/AR-24 restore cumulative F14 scope and add private PIN enrolment/recovery, staged abuse response and explicit follow-up evidence alongside earlier controls; AR-25 defines contact verification and independent patient/caregiver binding; the supplied positive verdict concerns v1.5 and is not independent approval of this revision.

The [system design](SYSTEM_DESIGN.md), [roadmap](ROADMAP.md) and [business operations](BUSINESS_OPERATIONS.md) remain the shared contracts. This document adds the channel without creating a second patient, wallet, appointment or clinical-record authority. Fixed consultation fees, callback behavior and charge criteria below are proposed defaults for owner review, not invented commercial terms.

## 1. Patient and partner journey

1. **Register or identify the patient.** Authorized staff creates/finds the permanent Jansetu patient ID with a phone contact or authorized caregiver. A family phone can serve several patients; the caller selects the patient. The phone is a contact/lookup hint, never the unique patient identity or spending authority. Staff-entered contacts start unverified; section3.4 defines confirmation and independent binding before credential use. Patients with no phone retain the assisted reception route and can receive a printed account reference.
2. **Confirm and fund the right account.** Staff confirms a minimally disclosed patient identity and amount, records the patient beneficiary separately from payer/caregiver, obtains approval, and issues an SMS or printed receipt with a transaction reference. Partner top-up permission alone cannot reveal the patient's history, change their PIN or spend their balance. First-use PIN setup happens privately through section3.3; no cashier terminal or receipt contains the patient's PIN.
3. **Call or request callback.** The patient calls a published Jansetu number, chooses a supported language and enters patient reference plus a private PIN, or uses an approved assisted-verification route. Caller ID or possession of a shared phone is insufficient. SMS is optional: keypad prompts, voice assistance and printed references must work without it. Callback support and toll-free versus normal call charges must be disclosed; an ordinary incoming number may still require the patient's mobile-network airtime. Clinical operations must provide urgent-help/referral routing before a payment gate; urgent requests cannot wait behind a recharge or ordinary consultation queue.
4. **Agree to the consultation.** Establish the patient/caregiver authority, consent and appropriate doctor/service. Announce the fixed service fee, any disclosed access charge and the policy for advice/referral-only outcomes and refunds, check availability, then atomically reserve funds and doctor capacity. Do not promise a prescription. Offer a bounded queue or consented callback when unavailable. Waiting, unanswered and failed-to-connect calls do not consume the consultation fee.
5. **Connect to a doctor.** Bridge the patient and verified practitioner through a telephony provider. Use the same appointment/encounter as an app consultation and show the authorized clinical context in the doctor dashboard. A telecom answer event is not evidence that a clinical consultation occurred. The clinician determines whether audio is adequate or a video/in-person review is needed. Offer a returning patient their previous practitioner when available and consented, or an explicitly explained wait/callback; another available doctor does not inherit follow-up prescribing eligibility.
6. **Finish or reconnect.** Under the approved charge policy, post one fee against the same consultation operation when the clinical service becomes chargeable. A dropped call resumes that operation during a disclosed reconnect window; it does not automatically create another fee. Uncertain outcomes enter reconciliation/manual review. Avoid per-minute consultation debits and automatic clinical-call termination when a running balance reaches zero.
7. **Deliver the outcome.** Provide a minimal receipt/status by the chosen channel and authorized prescription access/printing. Avoid sensitive details in shared-phone SMS or spoken prompts. A pharmacy/lab/hospital receives clinical documents only under the relevant patient authority and release policy. Recharge/referral attribution is separate from care consent and does not automatically create commission.

## 2. Top-up funding, balance and reversals

Customer balance liability, partner funds, partner cash collections and doctor/business payables are distinct. Recording cash at a counter is not proof Jansetu received money. Proposed first-pilot funding is a prefunded partner allocation or confirmed digital collection under an approved funds-flow contract. If a receivable/credit facility is later selected, it needs explicit limits and settlement terms; there is no implicit unlimited partner credit.

### Funding choice and existing reuse (AR-21)

P1 must select the model before F14 is sized; fund custody, settlement and operating approval still gate any live use. The requested reloadable patient balance remains in scope. The Partner-paid consultation option is a narrower route, not a replacement silently selected by this document.

| Model | Account and care boundary | Reuse / unresolved work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Partner-paid consultation | Debit partner funds for one identified patient's consultation; no unspent patient credit is created by that operation | Existing pharmacy service is a primitive. Partner funds, collection and doctor settlement still need review; no automatic RBI exemption is claimed |
| Consultation-only balance | Reloadable patient credit restricted to approved consultation charges; a purpose-restricted pocket under the shared wallet authority, with its own available/held counters | Preserves the requested top-up journey without authorizing other marketplace spending; identity, funding, expiry/reversal and access rules need P1 approval |
| General patient wallet | Credit the approved account holder's general balance; each admitted use and payer/beneficiary relationship is defined explicitly | Existing customer wallet code is reuse material, not approval for universal third-party spending; scope, migration and financial obligations require P1/D1/D2 review |

`PharmacyWalletService.payConsultation` already checks a linked pharmacy/patient and transactionally debits the pharmacy wallet, records a movement and conditionally marks the booking paid. The paid-state guard can roll back a competing debit; saying it has no duplicate protection would be inaccurate. However, it accepts `req.amount`, has no dedicated payment-body schema or durable command/result idempotency, and the inspected pharmacy-wallet router exposes only balance/history/top-up. This is unrouted, unverified pharmacy-only reuse, not a live phone/payment feature or a lab/hospital wallet. It uses app-account-linked bookings, so M1 identity work remains necessary. [Service](../../src1/modules/pharmacyWallet/services/index.ts), [router](../../src1/modules/pharmacyWallet/routes/index.ts), [validation](../../src1/modules/pharmacyWallet/validation/index.ts).

Before any exposure, derive the payable fee/currency and remaining obligation on the server from authorized booking/price evidence; validate allowed inputs, recheck ownership/eligibility and expected state in the transaction, and return the original result for an authenticated duplicate command. Retain the existing paid-state guard. Do not enable a route, accept a caller-selected fee or generalize pharmacy funding to labs/hospitals through this documentation update. All chosen models use the same financial owner; a new pocket cannot become an independent balance writer.

Each top-up has a stable economic-operation ID, patient beneficiary, payer, currency/unit, collecting business/branch/staff, funding source, receipt and status. In one transaction, a prefunded top-up conditionally reduces that partner's available allocation and credits the patient liability with matching movements and outbox/result. Gateway funding credits only after captured/settled evidence according to the selected model. Duplicate clicks, callbacks and crashes cannot mint additional value. Declined or unknown funding cannot appear as spendable balance.

Resolve the beneficiary to the approved account holder/payer through the M1 identity crosswalk and caregiver authority. Existing `userId` wallets cannot simply be relabelled with a phone or patient ID; account ownership and any legacy balance mapping need reviewed evidence.

Consultation funds have available/reserved/spent/released states. Available balance excludes active reservations; competing app and phone spends use guarded updates on the same account. Repeated calls for an active consultation resolve that operation rather than reserve twice. Reservation expiry must reconcile whether a bridge or consultation is active; a delayed telecom callback cannot revive a released reservation or charge a new session. Active clinical sessions use bounded leases and an operator escalation path, not silent expiry or an indefinite money hold.

The authoritative account/pocket keeps posted and held minor-unit counters with `available = posted - held` and `posted >= held >= 0`. Reserve guards available and increases held; release decreases held once; capture of that hold decreases posted and held once. Ordinary spend, transfer and other reservations guard available on the same document, not a sum of hold rows read from a snapshot. The operation, hold/movement, booking state and outbox/result commit together. App spending cannot consume phone-held funds, and a restricted pocket cannot fund a disallowed service.

Unspent top-up reversals and refunds preserve the original funding/beneficiary references. Partner removal cannot erase customer liability. Refunds to wallet, gateway or cash share the captured-payment refund budget described in design section 9.4; unknown external refunds keep their reservation until reconciled. Do not authorize a cash-out, transfer or new refund destination through this feature before D1/D2 and P1 below are resolved.

## 3. Architecture and provider contracts

`phoneConsultations` is a proposed application module in the existing modular backend. It coordinates patients/consents, booking capacity, billing/wallet operations and clinical encounter commands through their owners. A telephony port provides number/call bridging, callback/status lookup and termination controls; its adapter owns provider payloads, not patient balances or clinical release. An IVR is a voice menu reached through the telephone network; the patient does not need mobile data. A partner panel still needs the connected counter service.

Keep one consultation ID and stable provider call-leg mappings. Persist an outbound call intent before provider submission, and do not redial blindly after an unknown timeout. Signed/authenticated ordinary status events enter the durable inbox; replay and out-of-order handling are version/fence guarded. PIN-bearing input follows the transient verification exception below and never enters a raw event archive. Workers reconcile provider facts outside Mongo transactions. Provider-secret rotation, restricted egress, retention and redacted logs apply. Call lifecycle and charge lifecycle are separate: ringing/answered/disconnected cannot directly declare a completed encounter or debit the wallet.

Persist call/consent/charge metadata with restricted access. Do not record calls by default. Recording, if specifically selected, requires its own consent, purpose, access and retention decision; it is not a prerequisite for keeping the clinician's encounter notes. A top-up cashier cannot reset a patient PIN; recovery uses separately authorized, audited identity checks, including changed/recycled numbers.

### 3.1 PIN input, verification and abuse controls (AR-20)

Prefer an authenticated provider POST with keypad input in its body; do not accept PINs in URL/query/path. Verify the provider signature over bounded raw bytes in memory, verify the call/session binding, apply shared admission limits before expensive verification, check the PIN transiently and discard the digits. Persist only an allowlisted event/session reference and non-secret processed outcome. No raw PIN payload, keypad digits or replayable verification grant enters the inbox/outbox, dead letters, audit, traces, request dumps, exception bodies or recordings. Disable sensitive request capture at the edge, proxy, framework, APM and provider console; a plaintext URL can leak before application redaction. Provider retention/DTMF behavior is unverified until P3 acceptance. Reject unsupported transports/configurations rather than archive the input. [OWASP logging guidance](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Logging_Cheat_Sheet.html).

Use a vetted slow password KDF with unique salt and an additional secret pepper/key in a separately access-controlled vault/HSM/KMS-backed secret, outside MongoDB and its backups. Store only the approved keyed verifier and key version, not a separate unkeyed verifier or plaintext PIN. Define key outage, rotation and compromise/reset procedures before release; hashing alone does not solve a short PIN's small search space. A database-only theft test must not permit local PIN verification without the separate key; this does not claim safety after key/service compromise. [OWASP password storage guidance](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Password_Storage_Cheat_Sheet.html).

Issue random non-sequential references with check digits and enough entropy for the expected population, tested for keypad usability. A check digit detects input errors and is not authentication. Deny common/repeated/sequential PIN choices. Enforce shared, multi-instance attempt/call budgets per patient/account, session, calling number/risk signal, ingress/provider and globally; caller ID is not a trusted identity. Generic failures and minimal prompts limit account enumeration. A one-guess-per-account attack must trigger the global abuse response, not evade it. Apply admission limits before expensive verification. A global signal must not simply disable PIN checks for every patient: progressively slow/challenge risky attempts, limit abusive sessions/provider sources and reserve bounded verification capacity for established sessions or independently verified-contact callback. A protected lane still requires authentication, consent and spending authority; caller ID alone cannot qualify it. Existing authorized encounters and urgent-help/controlled assisted routes remain available within engineered capacity. KMS/key outages still fail closed for PIN verification; throttling is not permission to skip the verifier. Any staff-assisted fallback is separately authorized, audited and throttled; it cannot bypass the account's spending/consent authority or give a cashier PIN-reset rights.

Successful verification gives a short-lived patient/purpose/consultation-bound authorization through the backend's identity owner, not an unscoped wallet-spend flag. Deduplicate provider retries by safe event/session identity without retaining the submitted digits. Repeated input must not mint multiple grants or reset attempt limits. Synthetic end-to-end checks must cover provider request/retention settings as well as all local persistence and observability surfaces; no provider has yet been certified to meet this contract.

The generic inbox payload-hash rule does not apply to PIN-bearing bytes. An unkeyed digest of short keypad input can itself enable guessing; retain no such fingerprint. Any retained conflict/receipt hash covers the allowlisted sanitized envelope only, with attempt identity and verifier state controlling replay.

### 3.2 Prescribing and referral-only outcomes (AR-22)

The cited 2020 guidelines distinguish medicine lists by actual consultation mode/type: List O covers any mode; first-consult List A requires video; eligible follow-up refills/add-ons have defined A/B scope; Schedule X and NDPS-listed substances are prohibited remotely. This is a policy baseline, not a medication catalogue or an assertion of current clinical certification. [Guidelines sections3.6-3.7.4 and matrix](https://esanjeevani.mohfw.gov.in/assets/guidelines/Telemedicine_Practice_Guidelines.pdf).

Before release, P2 approves a versioned policy/catalogue against then-applicable rules and separately assesses audio suitability. Store actual audio/video mode, first versus eligible follow-up, current and prior practitioner IDs, prior encounter ID/date/mode, condition and policy version. The cited baseline requires the same practitioner within six months of a prior in-person consultation for the same condition, without disqualifying new symptoms or lost clinical context. Missing/changed practitioner, non-in-person or missing prior evidence, expired window or a different condition prevents automatic follow-up classification. Clinical owners approve date-boundary rules; do not silently equate six calendar months to 180 days. [NRCeS guideline rendering](https://www.nrces.in/standards/telemedicine-practice-guidelines). The doctor UI explains disallowed choices; backend checks block prohibited or mode/type-ineligible prescriptions even if a caller changes fields. Unknown classification or inadequate assessment routes to clinical review/referral, not a permissive default. A doctor using a web dashboard does not make a basic-phone encounter video, and a user-selected follow-up label cannot expand prescribing rights. Explain advice/referral-only outcomes and their fee/refund treatment before reservation; the clinician can choose referral without a prescription.

### 3.3 Private first-use PIN and recovery (AR-23)

Registration and funding do not activate a spend-capable credential. The patient sets the first PIN privately during their own IVR call or a backend callback to an independently verified patient/caregiver contact under section3.4. Both first-use paths enforce that binding before issuing a usable challenge or accepting setup. A caregiver may do so only under recorded authority for that patient and the relevant financial purpose. Partner accounts cannot set, read, choose, reset or recover a patient PIN, and no PIN is typed on a cashier terminal or printed on a receipt.

The service can issue a random, short-lived, single-use enrolment challenge bound to patient, verified contact, purpose and credential/contact version. If printed, use a separate disposable enrolment slip excluded from archived financial receipts/PDFs, panel history and logs. A cashier might see that slip: its code alone is insufficient. Also prove control of the independently verified contact, for example with a service-originated callback; caller ID is not that proof. The collecting partner cannot change that contact or reissue a usable challenge to a new contact without separately authorized identity checks. A missing independent contact uses the recorded caregiver/assisted identity route rather than an unverified PIN grant.

Only a protected one-time verifier and safe status/reference are stored. Enrolment/reset digits follow the same transient provider-input and no-recording/no-payload-fingerprint rules as PINs. Atomic expiry/version/unused checks consume the challenge once with the new keyed PIN verifier and credential version; concurrent/replayed attempts cannot activate twice. Rate-limit enrolment requests, challenge issuance, setup attempts, reset requests, reset attempts and contact changes by account, contact/session/source and staged global budgets. No unlimited recovery fallback exists.

Reset requires a new account-bound challenge plus verified-contact/approved identity evidence and any caregiver authority. Public demographic answers or a cashier's assertion alone cannot recover a credential. Merely requesting recovery must not change the PIN, invalidate valid sessions or lock out the victim. On completed reset revoke affected grants and older challenges, append secret-free audit and notify safe registered contacts by consented voice/SMS where available; notifications never include the PIN/challenge. Failed notification is retried and visible to support. Changed/lost contacts require a separate audited recovery decision, with notices to old/new safe contacts where possible. Preserve urgent-help access without granting unverified wallet authority. [OWASP recovery guidance](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Forgot_Password_Cheat_Sheet.html).

Before release, P3/security owners approve thresholds, time windows, protected-lane capacity and operator queues. Test that a legitimate verified-contact caller with the correct PIN completes verification during a defined cross-account spray below tested capacity. This is not an unlimited availability promise or a correct-PIN bypass of risk controls. [OWASP authentication/lockout guidance](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authentication_Cheat_Sheet.html).

No telephony provider, monthly minimum, per-leg cost, caller-number category or concurrent-call capacity is selected yet. Trial the provider's sandbox, event authentication, idempotency/lookup, language/keypad support, emergency/recovery behavior and total call-leg costs before committing the integration. Publish the support number only after end-to-end acceptance.

### 3.4 Contact verification and patient binding (AR-25)

A number entered by partner staff is **unverified**, including a legacy contact or a number with a generic OTP success. The identity owner records two separate facts: possession of the proposed contact and its approved binding to the patient or authorized caregiver. A successful call proves possession, not the beneficiary's identity. Neither caller ID, demographic answers, masked-number readback nor a cashier's assertion establishes that binding.

The service may place a rate-limited **contact-verification call** while the patient is present, with confirmation privately on the patient/caregiver-controlled handset. This unprivileged call contains no PIN setup code, clinical history or spending grant. It is distinct from a privileged **PIN-setup callback**, which an unverified contact cannot receive. Possession confirmation alone cannot issue an enrolment challenge, recover a PIN or admit a caller to the protected verification lane.

Before promotion to **independently verified**, require patient/caregiver confirmation and independently approved beneficiary/relationship evidence under the M1 identity policy. Use an already trusted patient credential or a separately authorized identity verifier checking approved evidence and caregiver scope; the collecting/registering cashier cannot approve their own submission by changing roles. A cashier answering their substituted number still fails this independent check. M1 identity/product/privacy and security owners must approve the evidence methods and assisted process; no ABHA, Aadhaar, smartphone or SMS is mandatory. If evidence is insufficient, retain a pending state and offer the audited caregiver/assisted identity route; urgent-help access continues without unverified wallet authority.

The identity owner alone commits the binding, recording patient/contact and credential versions, purpose, method, possession result, independent verifier/evidence reference, caregiver authority/consent and time. Minimize evidence storage. Issue usable enrolment challenges only against this current binding. The separate disposable setup slip shows the masked contact number; read it back in the chosen language for patient confirmation before activation and during account confirmation before funding. Readback detects mistakes/substitution and is not authentication. Funding a correctly identified beneficiary does not itself verify a contact or activate a credential.

Flag contacts matching staff numbers or reused across many unrelated patients for independent review before credential promotion. Apply bounded, privacy-safe correlation: do not expose other patients' identities, merge accounts or ban legitimate family/shared-phone use. A documented caregiver relationship can explain sharing; a staff member receiving care still needs independent approval. Contact replacement or evidenced recycling requires authorized re-verification and version invalidation of old binding/challenges/grants. Never inherit verification from a cashier edit or automatically suspend a valid binding merely because an unauthenticated person proposes a change. Recovery and protected-lane admission require the current approved binding plus their other controls.

NIST distinguishes identity proofing from subsequent authenticator binding; OWASP treats factor replacement as a sensitive recovery action. These inform the proposed separation above, not a claim of a NIST assurance level, compliant identity service or a selected provider capability. [NIST identity proofing and enrolment](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63a.html), [OWASP factor replacement](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Multifactor_Authentication_Cheat_Sheet.html).

### 3.5 Restore and recovery admission (AR-29/AR-30)

Restoring an old database cannot reactivate revoked caregivers/contacts, older PINs or consumed setup/reset challenges. A recovery generation outside the restored rollback domain invalidates old grants/challenges; new login or PIN setup also waits for current independent identity/authority evidence, rather than trusting restored contacts. Pause sensitive sends/bridges and new irreversible top-up/charge effects until admission/reconciliation; preserve approved urgent assistance without unverified spending authority. Recover partner cash/top-up obligations, held funds and consultation operation IDs using independently recoverable evidence or owner-approved exceptions. A missing post-backup row is not permission to credit/charge again. System15.1–15.2 and D9 gate restore acceptance before any phone pilot; they add no new M0 dependency on F14.

## 5. Required verification

- A staff-entered contact without patient confirmation cannot receive a PIN-setup callback, usable enrolment code, recovery grant or protected-lane privilege. An unprivileged, throttled possession probe does not activate any of these.
- A dishonest cashier enters their own number, copies the disposable code and answers a verification call: possession alone still cannot promote the contact or claim the patient. Require independent beneficiary evidence and a separate authorized approval actor; reject self-approval through another role.
- The disposable slip displays the masked current contact and the call reads it back; incorrect/replaced contact cannot use an old challenge. No full contact or secret enters archived financial receipts.
- Staff-contact matches and repeated unrelated-patient reuse are flagged before promotion; approved family/caregiver sharing remains usable without merging patients or revealing other identities. Test a legitimate staff-patient's independently approved exception.
- Recycled/changed contacts require fresh binding and invalidate affected old grants on authorized completion. A forged change/flag request cannot disable a legitimate patient's credential. Concurrent approval/change and callback replay enforce the same contact/credential version.
- P3 key-service outage, rotation and compromise/reset exercises have named security/DevOps and integration/operations approval; verification fails closed on unavailable keys while urgent care routing continues.

- Basic phone without app/data/SMS; shared-number patient selection, caregiver authority and PIN recovery; recycled number and guessing attacks; no history access from recharge rights.
- Synthetic PIN canary absent from provider/local logs, URL/query/path, inbox/dead letters, traces, recordings and backups after success/failure/replay. Database copy without the separate key cannot verify guesses; key outage fails closed for verification, with urgent-help routing available.
- Private enrolment and recovery: no partner account can set/read/reset a PIN; a copied printed challenge cannot claim the account without independent contact proof; expired, replayed and concurrently consumed codes fail safely. No PIN/challenge enters archived receipts, provider payload history or logs.
- Repeated setup/reset/issuance/contact-change attempts are throttled; an unauthenticated recovery request cannot invalidate a valid PIN/session. Completed change notifies registered safe contacts, revokes old grants/challenges and queues failed notifications without exposing secrets.
- One guess against each of 1,000 random patient references within the configured abuse window triggers the shared staged response across API replicas. During the controlled attack a legitimate verified-contact caller with a correct PIN still completes verification through reserved capacity; existing authorized encounters and urgent/assisted routes continue. Repeated webhook cannot reset limits or mint grants; fallback is also throttled.
- Prefunded allocation covers one top-up while two cashiers compete; replay/crash at each money boundary; wrong-beneficiary confirmation; chargeback/top-up-reversal and partner close reconciliation.
- Partner-paid consultation rejects caller-priced underpayment and unknown/invalid eligibility; authenticated duplicate returns the original result with one debit. Demonstrate each selected model's permitted uses without enabling unsupported lab/hospital wallets or substituting sponsorship for requested top-ups.
- App and phone compete for the last available funds and doctor slot; no doctor, no answer, callback failure, doctor rejection and consent withdrawal release safely without a consultation fee.
- With 500 posted and 300 held, a 300 ordinary debit fails; hold capture/release and a competing debit reconcile exactly once with no negative available balance. Values are synthetic minor-unit examples, not consultation prices.
- Duplicate/reordered answer/end events, outbound-create timeout, missing end event and worker crash: one active bridge/charge, no premature debit, bounded recovery and no repeated fee on reconnect.
- Clinical charge evidence versus telecom answer; disputed/unknown completion is reconciled rather than inferred from duration. Approved refunds compete across wallet/card/cash under one source budget.
- Audio-first versus eligible follow-up/video fixtures, same versus changed practitioner, prior in-person versus audio/video-only encounter, six-month boundary, new condition/symptoms or missing context, prohibited/unknown medicine, forged mode/type and referral-only outcome: backend denies invalid prescribing, UI explains the result and fee/refund behavior matches the announced policy.
- Set a separate concurrent-call target, call setup/queue/reconnect SLO and budget after the provider trial. The canonical 100 HTTP RPS profile remains unchanged; it does not mean 100 simultaneous calls. Measure call-control callbacks, sustained call concurrency, audio/connect quality, provider failure recovery and per-consultation cost separately.

These are unexecuted release criteria, not test results or evidence of a working helpline.
